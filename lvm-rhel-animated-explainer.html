How LVM builds storage in Red Hat Enterprise Linux

Logical Volume Management puts a flexible layer between your disks and your filesystems. Follow one web server's storage from two blank disks to a volume that grows while it is still mounted.

    The four pieces
    
  
    
    
    Physical volume (PV)
    A disk or partition that carries an LVM label. pvcreate writes the label. A PV does nothing useful until it joins a volume group.
    
    
    Volume group (VG)
    A pool made from one or more PVs. You add PVs to make the pool bigger, and logical volumes draw their space from it.
    
    
    Extent
    The unit LVM allocates, 4 MiB by default. A VG is a collection of physical extents, and a logical volume is a list of the extents assigned to it.
    
    
    Logical volume (LV)
    A block device built from a VG's extents. You format and mount it like a partition, but it can grow and it can span several disks.

    Details that trip people up
    
  
      
    
    • An LV has two equivalent names: /dev/vg_data/lv_web and /dev/mapper/vg_data-lv_web. Either works in commands and in /etc/fstab.
      • 
    
    • lvextend without -r grows only the volume. The filesystem stays the old size until you run xfs_growfs /srv/web (XFS) or resize2fs (ext4).
      • 
    
    • XFS can grow but cannot shrink. To make an XFS volume smaller you back up, recreate it, and restore.
      • 
    
    • The mount lasts until reboot unless you add a line to /etc/fstab, for example /dev/vg_data/lv_web /srv/web xfs defaults 0 0.
      • 
    
    • Tear down in the reverse order you built: unmount, lvremove, vgremove, pvremove.
